Monthly look at the 5 biggest **verified US** creator accounts from the radar (`automation/interaction.json`, kind `creator`): the 2 Reels with the most views in the last 30 days each. Hooks paraphrased, never copied verbatim. Done by Chrome module 6 (`14_chrome_modules.md`).
The Content Factory uses the learnings via `08_hooks.md` (section "Learnings from the competitor check").

## What to learn each month
- **Hooks:** which first lines pull the most views (concrete case? deadline? number? hot take?)
- **Formats:** talking head vs. screen recording vs. satisfying loop vs. carousel – which one wins in which category
- **Length:** best-performing lengths (Instagram web often doesn't show duration – note "n/a" then)
- **CTAs:** comment keywords, "link in bio", lead magnets – what do they offer for free?
- **Compliance:** how do creators phrase disclaimers without killing the vibe?
- **Gaps:** what nobody shows (e.g. real provision workflows, audit trails, code) – that's our angle

## Hypotheses from the German check (October 2026) – to verify in the US
The German check (sister account, top 5 German tax creators) produced three learnings. Check whether they hold in the US:
- **Concrete case beats code section:** top Reels started with a real case or a clear claim, not with the law. → For us: hook with a real spreadsheet mistake ("#REF! three days before Oct 15") instead of the tool feature.
- **Authority hooks pull:** "The tax office knows / may …" hooks performed best across accounts. → US version: auditor/reviewer perspective ("Your auditor asks: where does this number come from?"). **No IRS fear-mongering.**
- **Faces and guests scale:** podcast clips with well-known guests got 2–3× the views of solo Reels. → For us: collab Reel with a US CPA or creator ("CPA vs. code – who's faster?"), talking head + screen recording in split screen.
