# Fact check – 06 § 163(j) is back on EBITDA

**As of:** 2026-10-04 · **Post:** Thu 10/15/2026, 7:30 PM ET (carousel) + teaser, quiz (10/16), quiz answer (10/17)

## Legal basis
- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)**, Pub. L. 119-21, signed **July 4, 2025**.
- Change to **IRC § 163(j)(8)(A)(v)** (definition of adjusted taxable income, ATI) – OBBBA § 70303 (section number per secondary sources – verify in the statute text).
- Effective for **tax years beginning after December 31, 2024** (calendar year 2025 is the first year).
- Before (tax years beginning 2022–2024): ATI was computed **without** the add-back for depreciation, amortization and depletion → roughly EBIT. 2018–2021: with the add-back → roughly EBITDA. OBBBA restores the EBITDA-style add-back.
- Limit itself unchanged: business interest expense deductible up to **business interest income + 30% of ATI + floor plan financing interest** (§ 163(j)(1)).
- Disallowed business interest **carries forward indefinitely** (§ 163(j)(2)) – special rules for partnerships (excess business interest expense allocated to partners) and § 382 limits after ownership changes.
- **Small business exemption** (§ 163(j)(3) with § 448(c)): taxpayers under the gross receipts test (average annual gross receipts of the 3 prior years; $31M for tax years beginning in 2025 per Rev. Proc. 2024-40 – verify) are not subject to the limit. Tax shelters cannot use it.
- Further OBBBA changes for **tax years beginning after December 31, 2025** (not in the post, only background): Subpart F income (incl. § 956), net CFC tested income (formerly GILTI) and § 78 gross-up are excluded from ATI; new ordering rule for interest capitalization. Source: secondary sources (Grant Thornton, Clark Nuber, IRS Form 8990 instructions Rev. Dec. 2025) – verify.
- IRS: Form 8990 instructions (Rev. December 2025), FS-2025-09.

## Demo math, step by step
1. EBITDA $14,000,000 − D&A $4,000,000 = EBIT $10,000,000.
2. Old rule (2022–2024 style): ATI ≈ EBIT = $10,000,000 → limit 30% × $10,000,000 = **$3,000,000**.
   Interest $4,000,000 − $3,000,000 = **$1,000,000 disallowed** (carried forward).
3. New rule (tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2024): ATI ≈ EBITDA = $14,000,000 → limit 30% × $14,000,000 = **$4,200,000**.
   Interest $4,000,000 < $4,200,000 → **all $4,000,000 deductible**, $0 disallowed.
4. Difference: $4,000,000 − $3,000,000 = **+$1,000,000** deduction.
5. Federal tax effect: $1,000,000 × 21% = **$210,000** less federal tax (before state taxes).
6. Side note (not on the slides): the remaining room of $200,000 ($4.2M − $4.0M) could absorb disallowed interest carried forward from earlier years.

## Simplifications (say so if asked)
- ATI = tax concept (taxable income without business interest, NOL deduction, § 199A etc., plus D&A) – not book EBITDA. Tax depreciation (e.g. bonus) ≠ book depreciation.
- Ignores business interest income, floor plan financing interest, carryforwards, partnership rules, § 382, state taxes, the small business exemption.
- The $210,000 is mostly a timing benefit (carryforward would be used later) – said on slide 5.
